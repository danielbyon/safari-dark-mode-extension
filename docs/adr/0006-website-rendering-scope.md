# Website rendering is the first-release compatibility target

The owner chose authorized websites and web applications in Safari as the first-release rendering target. Separating ordinary webpage styling from document recoloring keeps the rendering evaluation coherent while preserving a distinct research path for additional content types.

## Boundaries

1. Darken website surfaces, text, links, forms, menus, and controls while preserving usability, including dynamically updated pages and web applications.
2. Preserve photographs, screenshots, video, artwork and content illustrations by default; interface icons and decorative graphics may adapt for readability. Preserve meaningful chart/canvas distinctions and semantic states (error/success, disabled/selected controls). The required image-dimming control defaults off and targets content images, including CSS background images where reliably classified, not interface icons or video. Page-color controls must not unintentionally transform preserved visual content. Classification and actual coverage are feasibility questions.
3. PDF document contents and other document-viewing surfaces are separate research and feature scope. This does not assert that recoloring them is impossible.
4. Safari's own interface and browser-owned pages are outside the website-darkening feature.
5. Rendering quality and compatibility remain the primary competitive goals; broad claims require actual validation against representative websites.
6. First public launch includes iPhone, iPad, and Mac with matching core capabilities. Platform conveniences may differ where OS availability requires it.
7. Include Safari standalone Add to Dock web apps in v1 on macOS versions where the required APIs are available and the behavior is verified. This is distinct from ordinary web applications in Safari tabs and must not raise the ordinary Safari browsing OS floor. Enablement and site permissions remain subject to the platform.
8. Onboarding explains why access to webpages is required and recommends Safari's all-websites permission for automatic coverage, but selective grants remain supported. Distinguish denied or unavailable Safari permission from preference-based inactivity. The app must not circumvent the platform's permission decisions.
9. Competitive rendering quality is a release gate. Evaluate the candidate engine against Dark Reader and Noir on approximately 30-50 representative websites, including complex dynamic content, native-dark sites, forms, SVG, embedded media, and frames where authorized. Compare readability, visual accuracy, functional correctness, bright flashes, responsiveness, and resource use. Use reproducible regression tests plus actual Safari/device testing where behavior is platform-dependent; avoid disproportionate qualification infrastructure. Establish the concrete corpus, metrics, and thresholds before claiming superiority. No benchmark result or final engine selection is implied.
10. Dynamic color transformation is the primary rendering-method candidate, not a selected implementation or engine dependency. Evaluate filter-based or static stylesheet alternatives only against observed compatibility failures. Ship an alternative method only if evidence shows it meaningfully resolves failures the primary method cannot reasonably address; do not maintain multiple rendering methods merely for feature parity. Keep rendering method conceptually separate from the chosen theme. The actual engine, alternative selection behavior, and performance/compatibility evidence remain unresolved.
11. Resolve saved preferences for the top-level website. Apply its effective appearance and activation across authorized embedded frames; do not apply independent saved rules for a frame hostname or path merely because it is embedded. Top-level inactivity prevents extension darkening throughout the page; separately authorized explicit preview follows ADR 0002. Preserve already-dark frame content when native-dark preservation applies, including reevaluation on dynamic changes. Each frame requires Safari authorization independently; never infer frame permission from top-level access. The embedded host's own rules apply when visited as a top-level website.
12. Launch quality has three independent gates: (a) no known critical rendering regressions in the approved supported test scope (unreadable primary content, broken essential controls, lost user input cannot be averaged away); (b) approved comparative targets against Dark Reader and Noir for rendering fidelity, usability, compatibility, responsiveness and resource use; (c) demonstrated meaningful advantages in scoped customization, predictable inheritance and everyday usability. Do not require superiority over both competitors on every individual page. Exploratory baselines may calibrate thresholds; approve and freeze the final corpus, criteria and coverage before final candidate qualification. Exploratory measurements alone are not a release pass.
13. Q78 establishes WCAG 2.2 AA as the minimum target for our own code, built-in themes, and controlled appearance transformations (ADR 0007). Qualification distinguishes a regression attributable to the product from an existing website defect, inaccessible content, or the accessibility consequences directly attributable to deliberately selected appearance values. A customized appearance is not a blanket exemption from unrelated product defects (ADR 0007). Representative website comparisons remain required to identify our regressions, but neither qualification nor customer guarantees assert full conformance of third-party websites.

14. Native dark mode is supplied by the website independently of our extension. Repeated detection after our transformation must not treat our own output as a website-controlled change or cause an enable/detect/disable oscillation. Qualify a light website that remains extension-darkened through repeated detection, then independently adopts its own dark appearance and triggers the accepted preservation policy (ADR 0002).
